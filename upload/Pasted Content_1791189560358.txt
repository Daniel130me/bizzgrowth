## Business Intelligence / AI Growth Operating System — Complete Clickable MVP Prototype

You are an expert product designer, UX architect, SaaS product strategist, and frontend engineer.

Your task is to create a **complete, high-fidelity, interactive prototype** of a new AI-powered Business Intelligence and Growth Operating System for growing small and medium-sized businesses.

This is NOT a landing-page-only exercise.

This is NOT a collection of disconnected dashboard mockups.

This must be a **deep, realistic, clickable end-to-end product prototype** that can be shown to:

- potential customers,
- business owners,
- investors,
- advisors,
- product reviewers,
- development teams,
- stakeholders considering whether the product should be built.

The people reviewing the prototype should be able to click through the product and understand how the proposed MVP works without anyone explaining every screen to them.

# 2. WHAT THE PRODUCT IS

The product is best understood as an:

> **AI Business Growth Operating System**

Its job is to understand a business, observe its performance, identify problems and opportunities, explain why something may be happening, recommend what the business should do next, help the business track those actions, and measure what happened afterward.

The central product loop is:

**Understand → Measure → Diagnose → Recommend → Act → Measure again**

Another way to express it is:

**Connect → Understand → Measure → Diagnose → Recommend → Execute → Measure again**

Everything in the prototype must support this loop.

This is much more than:

- analytics,
- dashboards,
- SEO software,
- AI chat,
- website analytics,
- CRM reporting,
- marketing reporting.

The product connects business information together and translates it into decisions.

The central promise is:

> **Understand what is happening in your business, why it is happening, and what you should do next.**

# 3. THE PROBLEM BEING SOLVED

Many growing businesses already have:

- customers,
- sales,
- marketing,
- websites,
- Instagram/Facebook activity,
- Google activity,
- WhatsApp enquiries,
- payment platforms,
- ecommerce stores,
- spreadsheets,
- reports,
- advertising campaigns.

Their problem is not necessarily lack of data.

Their problem is:

> **“We have information everywhere, but we don't know what it means.”**

Typical questions include:

> Why did sales fall this month?

> Which marketing channel is actually producing customers?

> We generated more enquiries, so why didn't revenue grow?

> Is our website helping or hurting us?

> What should we work on next?

> Where are potential customers being lost?

> Are we spending too much to acquire customers?

> Which products or services are actually driving growth?

> We implemented something last month. Did it actually help?

The product should answer questions like these.

# 6. PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY

The software must always distinguish between:

### What happened

Measured business information.

### What it may mean

Interpretation or diagnosis.

### What could be done

Recommendation.

### What actually changed

Action/change event.

### What happened afterward

Outcome.

This distinction is extremely important.

Do NOT design the platform as if AI magically knows everything.

---

# 7. TRUST AND EVIDENCE

This is one of the most important product principles.

Every important conclusion should make it possible to see:

- what data supports it,
- where the data came from,
- whether it is exact or approximate,
- whether it was entered by a user,
- whether it came from an integration,
- whether it is an estimate,
- whether it is AI interpretation,
- how confident the system is.

Use provenance labels throughout the product.

### Measured

Information directly retrieved from a source.

### User supplied

Information manually provided.

### Estimated

Approximate/calculated information.

### AI interpretation

A conclusion produced by reasoning over the available evidence.

Never visually treat these four categories as identical.

---

# 8. PROGRESSIVE INTELLIGENCE

Do NOT require a business to connect everything before receiving value.

The product should become more intelligent as more information becomes available.

Think of the progression as:

**Level 0 — Demo**

No information required.

**Level 1 — Private Diagnostic**

Business context + rough trends.

**Level 2 — Aggregate Data**

Monthly/weekly totals.

**Level 3 — Read-only Connections**

Connected analytics, marketing, sales or ecommerce sources.

**Level 4 — Deeper Intelligence**

Multiple sources allow better root-cause analysis.

**Level 5 — Actions and Change History**

The system observes what the business changed.

**Level 6 — Outcome Learning**

The platform begins understanding what historically works for this particular business.

This progression should be visible throughout the UX.

↓

### What we can access

Example:

### What we cannot do

Example:

### What this unlocks

Example:

“Website conversion analysis and better diagnosis.”

**Enter monthly totals instead**

where appropriate.

# 10. CONVERSATIONAL BUSINESS ONBOARDING

The onboarding experience should NOT be a rigid 12-step questionnaire.

The core concept is a conversational Business Context Engine.

The AI manages conversation.

Structured application logic manages the truth.

The conversational interface should progressively understand:

- business type,
- business model,
- what the company sells,
- who it sells to,
- primary goal,
- acquisition channels,
- buying journey,
- marketing trend,
- enquiry/traffic trend,
- customer/order trend,
- revenue trend where available,
- major recent business changes.

The system should act as though it is filling a hidden structured model.

The AI should ask:

> **What is the minimum next question that would increase useful understanding the most?**

Do not ask something the user has already answered indirectly.

Example conversation:

AI:

“Tell me briefly about your business and what you're trying to improve.”

User:

“We run a cleaning company in Lagos. Most customers come through Instagram and referrals. We've been spending more on Instagram lately but I don't think we're getting more customers.”

The system should understand:

- business = cleaning/service,
- location = Lagos,
- acquisition sources = Instagram + referrals,
- issue = possible marketing efficiency/conversion problem.

It should NOT then ask:

“What channels do customers come from?”

Instead ask something useful such as:

“Compared with the previous month, did the number of enquiries increase, decrease or stay roughly the same?”

If user refuses exact revenue:

“That's fine. I don't need the actual amount. Would you say revenue increased, decreased or stayed roughly the same?”

The system should know when enough evidence exists for a preliminary diagnosis and STOP asking questions.

# 11. FIRST WOW MOMENT

The first wow moment must NOT be:

“Here is your dashboard.”

The wow moment is:

> **“We noticed something about your business.”**

Example:

Marketing spend:

**↑ 40%**

Enquiries:

**↑ 50%**

Customers:

**↑ 5%**

Revenue:

**↑ 3%**

Diagnosis:

> **Your immediate growth constraint may not be lead generation.**

> Marketing activity and enquiries increased significantly, but customers barely increased. The biggest opportunity may be between enquiry and customer rather than simply increasing advertising spend.

Then show:

### What we know

Marketing ↑  
Enquiries ↑  
Customers almost unchanged  
Revenue almost unchanged

### What we think

The bottleneck likely occurs after enquiry generation.

### What we don't yet know

Whether the issue is:

- lead quality,
- response time,
- pricing,
- follow-up,
- sales process.

### Unlock a better answer

Add channel-level enquiry/customer totals or connect a marketing/sales source.

This concept should appear throughout the product.

6.0% → 9.1%

# 35. CONFIDENCE LANGUAGE

High confidence:

Strong evidence/data coverage.

Medium confidence:

Useful signal but some information missing.

Low confidence:

Not enough evidence to isolate the cause.

Low confidence is NOT an error.

Do not use red for uncertainty unless there is an actual risk/error.

---

# 36. DATA-QUALITY LANGUAGE

The platform should be transparent.

Examples:

> Sales data is 9 days old.

> 23% of customers do not have an acquisition source.

> August revenue was entered manually.

> Website traffic is measured from Google Analytics.

> The explanation below is an AI interpretation.

This transparency is part of the product value.

---

# 37. CHANGE LEDGER PRINCIPLE

The platform must never assume that its recommendation caused an outcome.

Track business changes.

An important result might happen after:

- campaign adjustment,
- pricing change,
- website redesign,
- promotion,
- staffing change,
- product launch.

If several things changed during the measurement window, say so.

Example:

> Sales increased 24% following three significant changes: increased Google advertising, lower pricing and a website redesign. There is not enough evidence to isolate which change contributed most.

Confidence:

Low.

This behaviour must appear in outcome/experiment screens.

---

# 38. ASK YOUR BUSINESS PRINCIPLE

AI answers must look grounded in company data.

Do not give generic answers such as:

“Try improving your social media strategy.”

Instead:

> Instagram produced 184 enquiries during September but only 11 became customers, compared with 24 customers from 62 Google enquiries. The main issue therefore appears to be conversion quality rather than enquiry volume.

Then:

> We do not currently know whether the lower conversion is caused by lead quality or follow-up speed.

This creates trust.

# 42. NOTIFICATIONS AND ALERTS ARE DIFFERENT

An **alert** is a meaningful business event.

A **notification** is a delivery mechanism telling a user that something happened.

Do not merge their concepts entirely.

Example alert:

“Instagram CAC increased 28%.”

Notification:

“You have a new high-priority business alert.”
