    72→    72→# 2. WHAT THE PRODUCT IS
    73→    73→
    74→    74→The product is best understood as an:
    75→    75→
    76→    76→> **AI Business Growth Operating System**
    77→    77→
    78→    78→Its job is to understand a business, observe its performance, identify problems and opportunities, explain why something may be happening, recommend what the business should do next, help the business track those actions, and measure what happened afterward.
    79→    79→
    80→    80→The central product loop is:
    81→    81→
    82→    82→**Understand → Measure → Diagnose → Recommend → Act → Measure again**
    83→    83→
    84→    84→Another way to express it is:
    85→    85→
    86→    86→**Connect → Understand → Measure → Diagnose → Recommend → Execute → Measure again**
    87→    87→
    88→    88→Everything in the prototype must support this loop.
    89→    89→
    90→    90→This is much more than:
    91→    91→
    92→    92→- analytics,
    93→    93→- dashboards,
    94→    94→- SEO software,
    95→    95→- AI chat,
    96→    96→- website analytics,
    97→    97→- CRM reporting,
    98→    98→- marketing reporting.
    99→    99→
   100→   100→The product connects business information together and translates it into decisions.
   101→   101→
   102→   102→The central promise is:
   103→   103→
   104→   104→> **Understand what is happening in your business, why it is happening, and what you should do next.**
   105→   105→

   288→   288→# 6. PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY
   289→   289→
   290→   290→The software must always distinguish between:
   291→   291→
   292→   292→### What happened
   293→   293→
   294→   294→Measured business information.
   295→   295→
   296→   296→### What it may mean
   297→   297→
   298→   298→Interpretation or diagnosis.
   299→   299→
   300→   300→### What could be done
   301→   301→
   302→   302→Recommendation.
   303→   303→
   304→   304→### What actually changed
   305→   305→
   306→   306→Action/change event.
   307→   307→
   308→   308→### What happened afterward
   309→   309→
   310→   310→Outcome.
   311→   311→
   312→   312→This distinction is extremely important.
   313→   313→
   314→   314→Do NOT design the platform as if AI magically knows everything.
   315→   315→

   317→   317→
   318→   318→# 7. TRUST AND EVIDENCE
   319→   319→
   320→   320→This is one of the most important product principles.
   321→   321→
   322→   322→Every important conclusion should make it possible to see:
   323→   323→
   324→   324→- what data supports it,
   325→   325→- where the data came from,
   326→   326→- whether it is exact or approximate,
   327→   327→- whether it was entered by a user,
   328→   328→- whether it came from an integration,
   329→   329→- whether it is an estimate,
   330→   330→- whether it is AI interpretation,
   331→   331→- how confident the system is.
   332→   332→
   333→   333→Use provenance labels throughout the product.
   334→   334→
   335→   335→### Measured
   336→   336→
   337→   337→Information directly retrieved from a source.
   338→   338→
   339→   339→### User supplied
   340→   340→
   341→   341→Information manually provided.
   342→   342→
   343→   343→### Estimated
   344→   344→
   345→   345→Approximate/calculated information.
   346→   346→
   347→   347→### AI interpretation
   348→   348→
   349→   349→A conclusion produced by reasoning over the available evidence.
   350→   350→
   351→   351→Never visually treat these four categories as identical.
   352→   352→
   353→   353→---

   355→   355→# 8. PROGRESSIVE INTELLIGENCE
   356→   356→
   357→   357→Do NOT require a business to connect everything before receiving value.
   358→   358→
   359→   359→The product should become more intelligent as more information becomes available.
   360→   360→

   362→   362→
   363→   363→**Level 0 — Demo**
   364→   364→
   365→   365→No information required.
   366→   366→
   367→   367→**Level 1 — Private Diagnostic**
   368→   368→
   369→   369→Business context + rough trends.
   370→   370→
   371→   371→**Level 2 — Aggregate Data**
   372→   372→
   373→   373→Monthly/weekly totals.
   374→   374→
   375→   375→**Level 3 — Read-only Connections**
   376→   376→
   377→   377→Connected analytics, marketing, sales or ecommerce sources.
   378→   378→
   379→   379→**Level 4 — Deeper Intelligence**
   380→   380→
   381→   381→Multiple sources allow better root-cause analysis.
   382→   382→
   383→   383→**Level 5 — Actions and Change History**
   384→   384→
   385→   385→The system observes what the business changed.
   386→   386→
   387→   387→**Level 6 — Outcome Learning**
   388→   388→
   389→   389→The platform begins understanding what historically works for this particular business.
   390→   390→
   391→   391→This progression should be visible throughout the UX.

   455→   455→# 10. CONVERSATIONAL BUSINESS ONBOARDING
   456→   456→
   457→   457→The onboarding experience should NOT be a rigid 12-step questionnaire.
   458→   458→
   459→   459→The core concept is a conversational Business Context Engine.
   460→   460→
   461→   461→The AI manages conversation.
   462→   462→
   463→   463→Structured application logic manages the truth.
   464→   464→
   465→   465→The conversational interface should progressively understand:
   466→   466→
   467→   467→- business type,
   468→   468→- business model,
   469→   469→- what the company sells,
   470→   470→- who it sells to,
   471→   471→- primary goal,
   472→   472→- acquisition channels,
   473→   473→- buying journey,
   474→   474→- marketing trend,
   475→   475→- enquiry/traffic trend,
   476→   476→- customer/order trend,
   477→   477→- revenue trend where available,
   478→   478→- major recent business changes.
   479→   479→
   480→   480→The system should act as though it is filling a hidden structured model.
   481→   481→
   482→   482→The AI should ask:
   483→   483→
   484→   484→> **What is the minimum next question that would increase useful understanding the most?**
   485→   485→
   486→   486→Do not ask something the user has already answered indirectly.
   487→   487→
   488→   488→Example conversation:
   489→   489→
   490→   490→AI:
   491→   491→
   492→   492→“Tell me briefly about your business and what you're trying to improve.”
   493→   493→
   494→   494→User:
   495→   495→
   496→   496→“We run a cleaning company in Lagos. Most customers come through Instagram and referrals. We've been spending more on Instagram lately but I don't think we're getting more customers.”
   497→   497→
   498→   498→The system should understand:
   499→   499→
   500→   500→- business = cleaning/service,
   501→   501→- location = Lagos,
   502→   502→- acquisition sources = Instagram + referrals,
   503→   503→- issue = possible marketing efficiency/conversion problem.
   504→   504→
   505→   505→It should NOT then ask:
   506→   506→
   507→   507→“What channels do customers come from?”
   508→   508→
   509→   509→Instead ask something useful such as:
   510→   510→
   511→   511→“Compared with the previous month, did the number of enquiries increase, decrease or stay roughly the same?”
   512→   512→
   513→   513→If user refuses exact revenue:
   514→   514→
   515→   515→“That's fine. I don't need the actual amount. Would you say revenue increased, decreased or stayed roughly the same?”
   516→   516→
   517→   517→The system should know when enough evidence exists for a preliminary diagnosis and STOP asking questions.
   518→   518→
