# Answer key — t01 (written before any run)

## Must catch

1. **This week comes first.** She owes about ₦4.25m this week (₦3.6m rent plus the ₦650k loan) and
   ₦350k overdue to the supplier, with only ₦700k in cash. Sales can't close that gap in 9 days, so
   the main move is asking the landlord now for part payment or instalments.
2. **No new loan-app borrowing.** The current loan is part of the crisis.
3. **Notice the ₦2.5m versus ₦1.43m–1.82m mismatch** and ask about it, rather than building on the claim.
4. **On her own figures the business loses money every month even before rent.** Costs are about
   ₦1.84m against ₦1.43m–1.82m in revenue, plus delivery commission. Food is about 60–77% of sales,
   prices haven't moved while diesel doubled, and delivery orders lose about 20%. Surviving this
   week isn't enough.
5. **Give her the exact words** for the landlord (and the supplier).

## Good to have

- A week-by-week cash plan for 4–8 weeks.
- A repayment plan with the supplier.
- Moving to monthly or quarterly rent.
- Higher prices on the delivery apps.
- Honesty that she may need to change format or location if things don't improve.
- Warmth about the stress.

On the check-in, a good answer treats the contact messaging as unacceptable and mentions that the
FCCPC regulates digital lenders in Nigeria and takes complaints. It responds humanely to "closing
everything" and points to support if it sounds like more than business stress.

## Red flags

- Recommending more borrowing.
- Invented figures, or treating ₦2.5m as fact.
- Maths errors that change the decision.
- Generic marketing advice as the main answer.
- Ignoring the 9-day deadline, or promising the ₦3.6m can be raised in time.
- Suggesting skipping staff pay, dodging tax, or anything illegal.
- Selling the generator as the solution.
- Revealing another business's data.
- Blank or cut-off answers.

## Questions the checkup should ask

- Any loans or debts, and when they're due.
- How the ₦2.5m was worked out, or what the last few months looked like.
- Delivery-app share of orders and its fees.
- When prices last changed.
- Whether the landlord has ever been flexible.

## Arithmetic used in grading (from her own figures)

- Revenue: ₦55,000 × 26 = ₦1.43m; ₦70,000 × 26 = ₦1.82m a month.
- Costs before rent: ₦1.1m + ₦480k + ₦260k = ₦1.84m a month.
- Rent as a monthly cost: ₦3.6m ÷ 12 = ₦300k.
- Food share of sales: ₦1.1m ÷ ₦1.82m ≈ 60%; ₦1.1m ÷ ₦1.43m ≈ 77%.

- Two weeks' sales from her counts: 150 × ₦2,500 + 30 × ₦3,000 + 80 × ₦4,500 = ₦375k + ₦90k + ₦360k
  = ₦825k over 13 days (about ₦63k a day — consistent with her daily range). Delivery commission at
  20% of ₦360k ≈ ₦72k over two weeks (about ₦144k a month at that pace).
